What yt-dlp reports, and why the header is gated on the query being a link
and Add all on a length yt-dlp named
==========================================================================

yt-dlp 2026.07.04, the same --flat-playlist --dump-json --playlist-items 1-3
the search always runs. Only the fields the header is built from are shown.

1. A playlist link. playlist_count is the whole list; n_entries is what
   --playlist-items left, so reading n_entries would report the
   truncation as the length - the bug the header exists to fix.
   https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL0-84-yl1fUnRuXGFe_F7qSH1LEnn9LkW
   {"id": "1F_OgqRuSdI", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 1}
   {"id": "7qHMXu99d88", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 2}
   {"id": "buMTH6ICnqk", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 3}

2. A typed query. ytsearchN: is a playlist to yt-dlp too, and every
   entry carries playlist_title set to the query itself. A header
   derived from the entries alone would announce every search as a
   playlist named after what was typed, which is why linked_playlist()
   answers None unless the query is a link.
   ytsearch3:bohemian rhapsody
   {"id": "fJ9rUzIMcZQ", "playlist_title": "bohemian rhapsody", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 1}
   {"id": "5zLnaNY58j8", "playlist_title": "bohemian rhapsody", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 2}
   {"id": "vbvyNnw8Qjg", "playlist_title": "bohemian rhapsody", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 3}

3. A link to one video. None of the three fields is there at all, so a
   linked video gets no header even though it is a link.
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F_OgqRuSdI
   {"id": "1F_OgqRuSdI", "playlist_title": null, "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": null, "playlist_index": null}

4. A link that carries a playlist - the form the address bar gives you
   partway through one. It answers as the playlist, not as the video
   named in it: same title, same count, and the rows are the
   playlist's first three rather than the pasted video's. Here the
   pasted video is the list's first, so it is among them; pasted from
   further in it would not be, which is what makes the header the
   thing that explains the rows.
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1F_OgqRuSdI&list=PL0-84-yl1fUnRuXGFe_F7qSH1LEnn9LkW
   {"id": "1F_OgqRuSdI", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 1}
   {"id": "7qHMXu99d88", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 2}
   {"id": "buMTH6ICnqk", "playlist_title": "Automate the Boring Stuff with Python", "playlist_count": 15, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 3}

5. Listings with no end to them. A Mix, a channel's videos tab and a
   bare @handle each answer with a playlist_title and with
   playlist_count null, which is what LinkedPlaylist.length_known()
   reads as no stated length: the header names the list and stops
   there, Add all is not offered, and every row keeps its own Add.
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fJ9rUzIMcZQ&list=RDfJ9rUzIMcZQ
   {"id": "fJ9rUzIMcZQ", "playlist_title": "Mix - Queen \u2013 Bohemian Rhapsody (Official Video Remastered)", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 1}

   {"id": "kijpcUv-b8M", "playlist_title": "Mix - Queen \u2013 Bohemian Rhapsody (Official Video Remastered)", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 3}
   https://www.youtube.com/@3blue1brown/videos
   {"id": "GlYgs6v2YfU", "playlist_title": "3Blue1Brown - Videos", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 1}

   {"id": "ldxFjLJ3rVY", "playlist_title": "3Blue1Brown - Videos", "playlist_count": null, "n_entries": 3, "playlist_index": 3}
